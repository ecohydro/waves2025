# Getting your news onto waveslab.org

The lab news page runs on what you tell it. Posting takes about a minute, and
you never have to touch the website.

## Three ways to post, all of them fine

1. **The form.** Click **Post lab news** at the top of `#waves-news`. Five
   questions, one of them required. Use this if you would rather be asked than
   compose something.
2. **Just type it** in `#waves-news`. A sentence is enough.
3. **React with 📰** on anything you already posted elsewhere, such as
   `#ucsb-lab`. This works on someone else's message too, so you can put up a
   colleague's news for them.

All three reach the same place. The form gets you a better first draft because
nothing is left out; free text gets picked up just as reliably and may come back
with one question.

## What is worth posting

Awards and fellowships. A paper accepted or out. A talk, poster, or conference
session. Fieldwork, a new deployment, a site visit. A defense or a milestone.
Someone joining or leaving the group. Press, a podcast, an outreach event. A new
grant. If you are unsure, post it. Sorting it out is not your job.

## What to include

One or two sentences of what happened is enough. Anything else you add makes the
draft better:

- when it happened, if it was not today
- who else was involved
- a link, to the paper, the program, the funder, the event
- a photo, attached to the message

You do not need a headline, you do not need to write it in the third person, and
you do not need to describe the photo. The draft, including the image
description the site needs for screen readers, gets written for you.

## What happens next

Within a working day you get a ✅ on your message and a reply in the thread
telling you what was drafted. The item goes into the site's editor as a draft,
where Kelly reviews it and publishes. Once published it appears at
[waveslab.org/news](https://www.waveslab.org/news), with your name on it and a
link to your profile page.

Nothing you post goes public on its own. Kelly publishes every item.

## Fixing something

Reply in the same thread. The reply you get shows the drafted headline, so if
the wording is off, say so and it gets updated automatically while the item is
still waiting for Kelly. Once it is published, ask Kelly.

If the reply says there is no profile page for you on the site yet, that is
worth fixing anyway, since it is what connects your news, your publications, and
your bio together. Ask Kelly to add you.

## What not to post here

Anything not yet public. A paper still under review, an award before the funder
announces it, a hire before the offer is signed. Wait for the announcement, then
post.
